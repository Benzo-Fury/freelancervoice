# Playbooks

Each playbook: the principle, the move, and example replies. Examples are adapted from scripts in the source videos (YouTube video IDs in brackets — youtube.com/watch?v=<id>). Adapt wording to the user's situation; don't copy blindly.

---

## 1. Discount request / "That number doesn't work for us"
**Principle:** Scope and price are the same thing. Respect their budget without disrespecting your value. [XZ-FLqN10u0, gcf-FRQNGGg, CRPV-EpamXU, hsTgAYskTn0]
**Move:** Keep the rate. Ask what they can live without, or ask for their ballpark and back into something simpler.

> Totally understand. That's our standard for something this scope and scale. Did you have a ballpark in mind? We can start there and back into something simpler — e.g. simplify the design, drop the premium pieces, fewer revision rounds, or extend the timeline. [u0xJHVx1CTM]

> Happy to get the number down — which part of the scope would you like to cut? Price and scope move together, so if one shrinks, the other does too.

> Totally fair question. That's the budget that's ideal for delivering the success metrics we outlined. If a lower budget is the priority, here's what we'd change about scope: [options]. [T7eT25tNJLU]

When the client stacks pressure (old invoices, "it's the market", a cheaper competitor, "more work next year"), from [gcf-FRQNGGg]:
- Answer each calmly: past prices were from years ago; save money somewhere, "not in the one place that determines if this works"; ask whether they liked the competitor's work.
- Name the real problem: often they promised someone internally a number. "So really we're talking about what you can defend in a room I'm not in — that's a problem I can help with."
- Reframe: "Right now you're not negotiating me down, you're negotiating your result down."
- Let them find the cut (e.g. push a deliverable to phase two). Give them a story for their boss about *results going up*, not cost going down.

Client names a fixed number ("we have $1,000"):
> Is the priority the things we just talked about, or to spend [$1,000]? I can optimise for one or the other, but not both. [v0_PO1PEd9g]
> We can design something that fits your budget, or keep the original scope and phase it out over time. Which path gets you to your goals faster? [T7eT25tNJLU]

## 2. "We found someone cheaper" / "I know freelancers who charge less"
**Principle:** There's always someone cheaper. Clients pay more for certainty. [89BCJwMUZtg, 3IB3SJvwkyQ, T7eT25tNJLU]
> If budget is the priority, you should definitely go with them. If the priority is anything else — [the result], getting it right first time — let's talk. (Cheap often means paying twice.) [T7eT25tNJLU]

> Makes sense to compare. Quick question so I point you the right way: is the main goal to stay under a certain budget, or to solve [problem] for good? If it's budget, I can take a few deliverables off and still get you [core result].

## 3. Free work / "It'll be great for your portfolio"
> I appreciate you thinking of me. I don't take on unpaid projects, but the result of this done properly will more than pay back the cost — happy to put together a quote so you can see what that looks like. [pg7sFdAgZVk]

## 4. Test / sample / spec work
**Principle:** Free work doesn't prove trust; it proves you'll work for free. Real clients test you with problems. [76ZM8MKOydw, IYNP3EoX-yA, mp6vPJhh0Ac, 95apNKqtbbE]
Pick ONE of these stances (don't combine them):
> **Paid test:** Sure — a quick test pass would be [$X], credited to the project if we go ahead.
> **No tests:** Thanks for asking. I don't do spec work, but here are a couple of similar projects and the results they got. My process starts with a pre-production phase, and we don't move into production until we're fully aligned, so you're never committing blind.

## 5. Scope creep / "Can you sneak in a few extras?"
**Principle:** Don't say no — say what it takes. Scope creep is an education problem. [-40AducVAEk, Rh8pNjVw1Fk, _vd2R9VpvOI, ANwmrGWaS5I]
> Absolutely. Just a heads up, this falls a little outside what we scoped. Happy to fold it in — it'd be [$X] and I could have it by [date]. Want it on the next invoice or billed separately? [FcQ3X2Spzuw]

> Love it. That's outside the current scope — happy to quote it for you.

Vague or "should be quick" requests: don't argue the "quick", just scope it.
> Happy to add that! It's outside what we scoped, so I'll quote it separately. Quick check so I price it right: [one scoping question]?

For many new ideas mid-project: "Happy to fold these in — we'll switch to hourly for any additional edits." (People get more decisive when it saves them money.)

## 6. Unlimited revisions / revision vs. redo
[pg7sFdAgZVk, L1_4kUONNDU, -1D9KJsMZng, GvaooQjkNgc]
> Sure. The quote includes two rounds of revisions; after that you can have as many as you like — we just switch to hourly.

> Revisions are definitely included — a revision is refining what I've built. What you're describing changes the brief itself, so that's new scope. Happy to quote it.

> Just a reminder, we've completed the revision rounds in the contract, so further changes will be billed separately. [6xy6NC6jl3A]

## 7. Rush / deadline moved up
**Principle:** Timelines can move, budgets move with them. Rush blocks you from other business even if you're free. [62HosTNGJeY, PTyFRMta7aY, s3jRiPRv9iU, -a-6k_zRwAA]
> Happy to hit the gas. That shifts our plan and capacity, so there's a rush fee of [20%]. Shall we proceed?

> That's doable — it'll affect either budget or quality. We can bring in extra hands (higher budget) or keep budget where it is and simplify the deliverable. Which would you like to prioritise?

## 8. Payment terms / late payment / "need the file now, we're Net-30"
**Principle:** You're a freelancer, not a credit card. [9dTjD63L6EQ, 02L-MP13FhE, F7ufAy54T_k]
- Terms you propose: 50% upfront, 25% on draft, 25% before delivery (or deposit + milestones).
- Final files release when payment clears: "Happy to send finals the moment the invoice is settled — if it helps, I can show you the final via a watermarked preview now."
**First check the contract.** If it set payment terms (deposit, on-delivery, Net-15), cite them. If it said nothing and the work is done, Net terms are weaker ground — be flexible now and fix the terms for next time.

After the work is done, client wants files now but pays Net-60:
> Thanks for confirming. As in our agreement, finals go out once the invoice is settled. If you need them today, you can pay invoice [#] now and I'll send everything the same day — or I can share a [preview/staging link] so your team can keep moving. Which works better?

Before the project (negotiating terms):
> On Net-60: That works if delivery runs on the same terms — I'd deliver 60 days after approval. Or we keep the timeline and move to 50% upfront / 25% on draft / 25% before delivery.

Late payment, friendly-firm:
> Hi [Name], quick nudge on invoice [#] for [$], due [date]. Can you confirm when it'll be processed? I'll hold [next milestone/delivery] until it's cleared so everything stays on track.

## 9. "What's your rate?" before scoping
**Principle:** Price without context is a guess. Quote comes last. [cUKT2kY3gYI, ZPKQrRWVr_0, BbPnl060qQc, 7dqMYzbW_ic, 0GtURCq8YrE]
> Happy to share numbers. First, I want to make sure I quote the right thing — what business result are you hoping this drives, and how will we measure success?

If pushed for a ballpark: "Without knowing more it'd be a wide range — something like [$5k–$20k]. A 15-minute call would narrow that right down." If unsure, quote a bracket with your real number at the low end. [Vkj9OwJjjTg]

## 10. "We don't have the budget"
**Principle:** Figure out can't vs won't. Scale the solution down, not the value. [3Ki9XV2riTY, dRgKIZ5tacI, tNDnvCZdphU, 2F43LbTV7iQ]
> Totally fair. Out of curiosity, what's this problem costing you each month in time, money or risk if it doesn't get solved? Sometimes the fix costs less than the problem, sometimes it doesn't — and that's okay too. If now's not the time, I can scale this down to [smaller version] so you still get progress.

## 11. AI objections
[nkL2F_6nWOw, QFSy92QwTc4, QvjWXHSRV4c]
> "Use AI and lower your rate" → I already use AI — it's part of why I can deliver more, faster. It doesn't replace the thinking, and the thinking is what you're paying for.
> "We can do this with AI" → Great, me too. AI handles the repetition; I make the decisions about what's worth building and why.

## 12. "We'll do it ourselves"
[JxEec3cF7q4]
> Totally an option. Want me to help you map what that'll cost? Who on your team would take it on, how long would it pull them off other priorities, and how confident are you it lands first time?

## 13. Ghosting after proposal / follow-ups
**Principle:** Don't chase — lead. Never "just checking in." [tAiH5hfE6PE, oQqqPRhPY_I, zqvTfPU1uoU, AaJuaecP-VI]
> Hi [Name] — I'm locking in my schedule for [month] and wanted to check whether your objectives for [project] have changed. Happy to adjust the proposal if so.
Prevention: present proposals live. "Absolutely, I'll get to work on that right after this. Let's book time so I can walk you through it before it goes to the team — Wednesday or Friday at 2?"
On a call: "If we could solve [X] to achieve [Y], would it make sense to move forward?" → recap goal, next step, send invoice while still on the call.

## 14. Stalling / "reviewing internally"
[R1-A-am3W1A, NV9IgnP5Qao]
> Totally fine to pause. Just want to make sure [goal] is still urgent on your side?
If still stalled: "Happy to pick things back up when it's a priority again. Here's the invoice for the ground we've covered so far."
"We'll think about it" → "Of course. Is there anything you're still unsure about that I can help clarify?"

## 15. Client blames you for delays they caused
[tplsRBLS44c] Calm facts, no blame:
> We're right on the schedule we agreed — notes were due on [date] and came in on [date], so delivery moves to [date]. Happy to look at ways to speed things up if needed.

## 16. Micromanagement
[7DP5zBilnyw, tX_tNrwVrFA]
> Totally, got the notes. Can I show you how these decisions ladder up to [main goal]? Want to make sure we're both seeing the same finish line.

## 16b. Client gives a note you think will hurt the work
[v0_PO1PEd9g]
> Yes, we can absolutely do it that way. However, [reason] — it's probably going to negatively affect [the result you're paying for]. Want me to try [alternative], or go ahead as requested?

## 17. "This isn't what we wanted"
**Principle:** When a client says something's wrong, they're usually right; when they say how to fix it, they're usually wrong. [cXxFdip1GoE]
> Thanks for being straight with me. Can we hop on a quick call? I'll walk through how each choice ties back to the strategy we approved, and we'll pinpoint exactly what's not landing so I can adjust.

## 18. Raising rates / discount for "future work"
[UjAer5jlt-w, XKhmxkpbyHg, nnMTrKaG_y4]
> Yes, that's my current rate — it adjusts as I book up and bring in new techniques.
> To existing clients: I love working with you, and wanted to give you plenty of notice: from [date] my rate for [service] will be [$]. Hope that's not a deal-breaker — if it is, I completely understand and I'm happy to recommend some people.
> "Discount now, lots more work later": Great to hear you're about to be busy! I don't discount individual projects, but if you'd like to lock in a six-month retainer, I can take [5%] off the total.

## 19. Underquoted / can't deliver
[sBCsGLmTGKM, qnBm-Ohe8Rg]
> As we're getting into this, it's more involved than we initially discussed, so we'll need to rethink budget. If that's not an option, I have some ideas to pare down the deliverable and still hit [target].
(If you can't deliver, subcontract it under your management rather than dropping the client.)

## 20. Delight / under-promise, over-deliver
[RWMDF3KdRc8, 5qtTO7JHy1c]
- Quote 6 days when you can do 4; deliver in 4.
- On early delivery: "I moved a few things around to get this to you sooner" + small bonus or a 2-minute walkthrough video.
- Deliver with intent: "This has been optimised for [result]. Let me know any thoughts or questions."

## 21. Referrals & repeat work
[el_OKpWGxAc, 2Xol-rLDWYc, 0dFwkW_xEWQ, dAOfktSRCc8, FJpS8MON__4]
> Ask before the finish line: We're on track to hit [metric] next week. If anyone in your circle needs the same, feel free to connect us — we'll treat them like family.
> Within a client's company: We did something really similar for [company]'s HR team — feel free to pass this along to yours. / Are any other departments working on something like this?
Best windows to re-offer work: before the first project ends, right after a big win for another client, and quarterly. Formula: lead with intel, back it with results, close with a 15-minute call. [FJpS8MON__4]
> Past-client check-in (3 lines): Just finished this project for a client and it made me think of you. You could be getting [result] too. Want to find time this week to hop on the phone?
